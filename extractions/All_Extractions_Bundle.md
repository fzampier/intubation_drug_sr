## 1.6 Risk of Bias (RoB 2)

| Domain | Judgement | Support |
|--------|----------|---------|
| D1: Randomization process | Low risk | Computerized random-number generator, blocks of 4, stratified by center, sequentially numbered identical sealed drug boxes. |
| D2: Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns | Single-blind: enrolling emergency physicians aware of allocation; ICU staff masked. |
| D3: Missing outcome data | Some concerns | mITT excluded 27.6% of randomized patients. ITT (n=650) showed consistent result (p=0.54). |
| D4: Measurement of the outcome | Low risk | 28-day mortality is objective. |
| D5: Selection of reported result | Low risk | Pre-registered NCT00440102. 28-day mortality was pre-specified secondary. |
| **Overall** | **Some concerns** | **D2 (partial blinding) and D3 (mITT excluding 28% of randomized patients).** |

## 1.7 Notes

- Primary endpoint of original trial was maximum SOFA score (not mortality).
- mITT analysis excluded patients dying before hospital and those discharged from ICU within 3 days — unusual and potentially biasing.
- Adrenal insufficiency significantly higher with etomidate: OR 6.7 (95% CI 3.5–12.7).
- Prehospital enrollment (EMS setting).
- For NMA: use 81/234 (etomidate) vs 72/235 (ketamine) for 28-day mortality.

- No NMBA was used in either arm — unique among included studies.
- ICU mortality used as best available (no 28-day mortality reported).
- For NMA: use 5/12 (etomidate) vs 8/10 (ketamine) for ICU mortality.
