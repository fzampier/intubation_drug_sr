**Key inclusion criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years
- Indication for orotracheal intubation in participating ICUs

**Key exclusion criteria:**
- Intubation during cardiac arrest
- Suspected or confirmed intracranial hypertension
- Bradycardia (HR <50 bpm) or AV block without pacemaker
- Pregnancy
- Known allergy to study drugs (lidocaine, fentanyl, propofol, ketamine, rocuronium)

**mITT exclusions (post-randomization):**
- Never intubated: 8 (2 ketamine, 6 propofol)
- Legal representative unavailable for consent before death: 15 (8 ketamine, 7 propofol)
- Refused consent: 9 (4 ketamine, 5 propofol)

*Note: 515/722 screened patients excluded, primarily due to unavailability of study team (46.3%).*

## 6. Risk of Bias (RoB 2) — for hospital mortality outcome

| Domain | Judgement | Support |
|--------|----------|---------|
| D1: Randomization process | Low risk | Centralized web-based randomization (REDCap), variable block sizes, stratified by site and vasopressor use. Allocation concealment by automated system. Baseline characteristics reasonably balanced (note: heart failure 30% vs 13%, SAPS 3 66 vs 60 — some imbalances). |
| D2: Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns | Open-label (drugs visually distinct). ICU team managing patients knew allocation. Could influence vasopressor titration, sedation choices, and other co-interventions. Outcome assessors and statisticians blinded. Adherence excellent (99%). |
| D3: Missing outcome data | Some concerns | mITT excluded 32/207 (15.5%) post-randomization: 15 died before consent available (8 ketamine, 7 propofol — informative exclusion), 9 refused consent, 8 never intubated. Primary outcome missing for 5/175 (2.9%). Exclusions broadly balanced but the 15 who died before consent is concerning (informative censoring). |
| D4: Measurement of the outcome | Low risk | Hospital mortality is objective. Patients followed to discharge or censored at day 60. |
| D5: Selection of reported result | Some concerns | Primary outcome was MAP (pre-registered). CV collapse and 7-day mortality were post hoc (not on clinicaltrials.gov before first patient, though added in protocol paper before data lock). Hospital mortality was pre-specified as tertiary/exploratory. Protocol paper published before analysis. |
| **Overall** | **Some concerns** | **D2 (open-label in ICU setting where clinicians manage vasopressors), D3 (mITT excluding 15.5% including 15 deaths before consent).** |

## 7. Notes

- **Only trial comparing ketamine (esketamine) vs propofol** in the review. Provides the critical propofol edge for the NMA network.
- **Esketamine** (S-ketamine enantiomer), NOT racemic ketamine. At 2 mg/kg, this is a high equivalent dose (~4 mg/kg racemic ketamine potency for NMDA antagonism). For NMA, mapped to the ketamine node.
- **Very high mortality** — hospital mortality ~55% overall. Authors attribute this to the high-severity ICU population in a Brazilian setting (SAPS 3 ~63, SOFA ~7.5).
- **No 28-day mortality reported.** For NMA, must use hospital mortality (censored at day 60). Authors estimate ~47% at 28 days for both groups combined.
- **Primary outcome was hemodynamic** (lowest MAP in 10 min), NOT mortality. The MAP difference (6 mmHg favoring ketamine) was statistically borderline (p=0.050) and transient — not sustained at 1 hour.
- **Mortality numerically favored propofol** at all timepoints (7-day, ICU, hospital) — all non-significant. Authors note this is 1.4 SD from null and may be chance. The consistent direction is notable.
- **CV collapse lower with ketamine** (22% vs 33%), driven by vasopressor escalation at 2 min — but this is a TERTIARY (post hoc) outcome.
- Imbalance in heart failure at baseline (30% ketamine vs 13% propofol) — could confound mortality.
- **Standardized intubation protocol** with checklist, premedication (fentanyl), and universal rocuronium — very strict protocol.
- First-pass success lower than other trials (~75%) — likely reflects resident-heavy intubation (78% by residents) in severely ill patients.
- Deferred consent process — ethical concern that 15 patients who died before consent were excluded (informative censoring).
- For NMA: use **55/91 (esketamine/ketamine) vs 42/84 (propofol)** for hospital mortality. Consider sensitivity analysis excluding this study given: (a) hospital vs 28-day mortality, (b) esketamine vs racemic ketamine, (c) high baseline mortality.
