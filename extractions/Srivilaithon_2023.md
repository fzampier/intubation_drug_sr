## 7. Notes

- **Sepsis-only population** — all 260 patients had suspected sepsis (Sepsis-3). This is the only trial in our review restricted exclusively to sepsis.
- Elderly population: mean age 71.9 years — oldest cohort in the review.
- **Retrospective registration** is a notable concern. Trial ran March 2019–December 2020; registered February 2021.
- Striking finding: 43.9% of etomidate patients needed vasopressors within 24h vs 17.7% ketamine (P<0.001). This contrasts sharply with Casey 2025 where ketamine had MORE peri-intubation vasopressor use.
- The vasopressor finding likely reflects **adrenal suppression** from etomidate, especially prominent in this sepsis-only cohort, manifesting over 24 hours (not immediate peri-intubation effect).
- Corticosteroid use also significantly higher with etomidate (14.6% vs 5.4%), consistent with adrenal suppression.
- Post-intubation hypotension (immediate, SBP <90 or MAP <65) did NOT differ — suggesting that the hemodynamic difference manifests over hours, not minutes.
- NMBA use differed significantly (more in ketamine group) — unclear if protocol-driven or clinician choice.
- No SOFA scores reported beyond delta SOFA at ED. No APACHE.
- Single-center in Thailand — may limit generalizability.
- For NMA: use **25/130 (etomidate) vs 35/130 (ketamine)** for 28-day mortality.
