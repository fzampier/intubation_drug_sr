## 6. Risk of Bias (RoB 2) — for mortality outcome

| Domain | Judgement | Support |
|--------|----------|---------|
| D1: Randomization process | Some concerns | Computer-generated randomization with variable block sizes and sealed opaque envelopes. Independent statistician prepared sequence. Baseline characteristics broadly balanced, but some numerical imbalances (stroke 32.5% vs 20%, lactate median 3.6 vs 3.2). |
| D2: Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns | Single-blind (drug preparer unblinded; intubator and outcome assessors blinded). Treating physician aware of allocation could influence subsequent care decisions (e.g., corticosteroid use was 15% vs 5%). NMB use was significantly higher in ketamine group (77.5% vs 65.0%, p=0.04). |
| D3: Missing outcome data | Low risk | All 80 patients analyzed on ITT basis. No withdrawals. |
| D4: Measurement of the outcome | Low risk | 28-day survival/mortality is objective. |
| D5: Selection of reported result | Some concerns | No trial registration reported. Primary outcome was 24-hour survival (not 28-day mortality). Multiple outcomes reported. Small study published in a low-impact journal. |
| **Overall** | **Some concerns** | **Single-blind with unblinded drug preparer; no trial registration; primary outcome was 24h survival (not the timepoint we use). However, 28-day survival was pre-specified secondary.** |

## 7. Notes

- Sepsis-only population (Sepsis-3 criteria) — one of only 2 included studies restricted to sepsis (with Srivilaithon 2023).
- Elderly population (mean age ~72 years).
- Vasopressor use within 24h was significantly higher with etomidate (45% vs 17.5%, p<0.001) — striking finding that authors attribute to etomidate-induced adrenal suppression.
- Corticosteroid use also significantly higher with etomidate (15% vs 5%, p=0.01).
- No cortisol levels were measured directly.
- No trial registration number reported — potential concern for selective reporting.
- Small single-center study (N=80) with limited power for mortality.
- Published in a low-impact Indian journal (SSR-IIJLS). Accepted August 2025.
- 28-day mortality: etomidate 8/40 (20%) vs ketamine 11/40 (27.5%) — numerically favors etomidate but not significant (p=0.09).
- For NMA: use 8/40 (etomidate) vs 11/40 (ketamine) for 28-day mortality.
